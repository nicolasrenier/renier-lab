Research

Exploring Brain Plasticity

Understanding how neuronal and vascular networks remodel to shape brain function, behavior, and individuality.

Our Vision

We investigate the relationship between the neurovascular unit plasticity and brain functions. We seek to understand how internal and external factors shape neural and vascular networks, and how plastic adaptations of those networks can in return shape behaviors and neuronal functions.

To tackle these questions, our team has developed expertise across three domains: advanced tissue clearing and light-sheet microscopy for mapping cerebral networks; Systems neurosciences and mouse behaviors (opto and chemogenetics, fiber photometry); and spatial and single cell transcriptomic.

Research Theme

Neurovascular Plasticity

We developed the TubeMap pipeline for large-scale reconstruction of the vascular network in the whole brain, enabling the creation of vascular graphs spanning approximately 300 meters with around 4 million blood vessels.

Our work revealed that brain regions have fundamentally different vascular structures that develop post-natally. We found that the structure of those network change throughout life, interacting with shifts in neuronal acticity levels. We are now investigating how the vascular system remodels during pregnancy, discovering region-specific changes in vessel density and branching that are essential for maternal behavioral adaptations.

Research Theme

Behavioral Flexibility & Individuality

We use pregnancy in female mice as a model of behavioral transitions, focusing on how nesting and pup care behaviors adapts during this period. Using whole-brain mapping and fiber photometry, we identified CART+ neurons of the Edinger-Westphal nucleus as key drivers of parental nesting, acutely sensitive to progesterone.

We also uncovered how inter-individual variations in innate behaviors emerge from seemingly identical genetic contexts, linking the activation of specific brain regions to stable individual differences.

Research Theme

Post-natal Development and Plasticity

We study how feedback mechanisms within the somatosensory system influence the dynamics of long-range projections from cortex to brainstem. We look at how intrinsic factors and experience shape the maturation of late projections, those who develop after birth. This could unlock our understanding on how axon guidance works in the crowded post-natal brain.

We also generated the first 3D neuroanatomical developmental atlas of the postnatal brain using tissue clearing and light-sheet microscopy, enriched with spatial transcriptomic data to contextualize cellular environment shifts during development.

Research Theme

Vascular-Immune architecture of brain tumors

We are studying the cellular architecture of patient tumors, in particular Glioblastomas, using our 3D imaging approach.

In particular, we strive to leverage these technologies, combined with advanced molecular phenotyping, to better describe the 3D organization of these tumors, how it correlates with prognosis and how it is modified by treatments.

Technology

Our Imaging Approach

We develop and advance tissue clearing and light-sheet microscopy methods for unbiased, whole-brain mapping at cellular resolution.

Light-Sheet Microscopy

High-throughput volumetric imaging of intact cleared organs, capturing the 3D organization of cells and networks.

Whole Brain Mapping

Our ClearMap software enables automated cell detection, mapping, and quantification across entire brain volumes.

Vascular Reconstruction

TubeMap pipeline for complete reconstruction of cerebral vasculature, producing graphs with millions of branch points.
