Research

Exploring Brain Plasticity

Understanding how neuronal and vascular networks remodel to shape brain function, behavior, and individuality.

Our Vision

We investigate the bidirectional relationship between the neurovascular unit and brain functions. We seek to understand how internal and external factors shape neural and vascular networks, and how plastic adaptations of those networks can in return shape behaviors and neuronal functions.

To tackle these questions, our team has developed expertise across three domains: advanced tissue clearing and light-sheet microscopy for mapping cerebral networks; the study of parental behaviors and neuromodulation; and the investigation of neuro-vascular and neuro-immune interactions.

Research Theme

Neurovascular Plasticity

We developed the TubeMap pipeline for large-scale reconstruction of the vascular network in the whole brain, enabling the creation of vascular graphs spanning approximately 300 meters with around 8 million branch points.

Our work revealed that sensory and motor cortical regions have fundamentally different vascular structures across their layers. We are now investigating how the vascular system remodels during pregnancy, discovering region-specific changes in vessel density and branching that are essential for maternal behavioral adaptations.

Research Theme

Behavioral Flexibility & Individuality

We use pregnancy in female mice as a model of behavioral transitions, focusing on how nesting behavior adapts during this period. Using whole-brain mapping and fiber photometry, we identified CART+ neurons of the Edinger-Westphal nucleus as key drivers of parental nesting, acutely sensitive to progesterone.

We also uncovered how inter-individual variations in innate behaviors emerge from seemingly identical genetic contexts, linking dopamine receptor neurons in the olfactory tubercle to stable individual differences in nesting architecture.

Research Theme

Structural Axonal Plasticity

We study how feedback mechanisms within the somatosensory system influence the dynamics of long-range projections from cortex to brainstem. We uncovered a bilateral feedback projection that initially projects equally to both sides but later develops a contralateral preference.

This work reveals how neuronal activity and action potentials could be locally integrated in axons to control guidance decisions, with implications for understanding how sensory processing is fine-tuned by the brain's feedback mechanisms.

Research Theme

Developmental Brain Atlas

We generated the first 3D neuroanatomical developmental atlas of the postnatal brain using tissue clearing and light-sheet microscopy, enriched with spatial transcriptomic data to contextualize cellular environment shifts during development.

Our analysis revealed a three-phase trajectory of brain vascularization: initial vascularization driven by brain growth, vascular densification driven by neuronal maturation, and a late phase of refinement driven by neuronal activity. This work challenges existing paradigms about vascular development.

Technology

Our Imaging Approach

We develop and advance tissue clearing and light-sheet microscopy methods for unbiased, whole-brain mapping at cellular resolution.

Light-Sheet Microscopy

High-throughput volumetric imaging of intact cleared organs, capturing the 3D organization of cells and networks.

Whole Brain Mapping

Our ClearMap software enables automated cell detection, mapping, and quantification across entire brain volumes.

Vascular Reconstruction

TubeMap pipeline for complete reconstruction of cerebral vasculature, producing graphs with millions of branch points.
